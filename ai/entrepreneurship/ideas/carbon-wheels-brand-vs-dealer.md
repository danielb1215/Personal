# Idea: Carbon Wheels — Own Brand vs. Official Dealer

## Status
> ⚠️ **Own brand: NOT RECOMMENDED** — fails on capital, liability, and 2026 tariffs.
> 🔍 **Official dealer (Colombia, presale model): worth a 30-day demand test** — but note it conflicts with the full-time-travel goal.

## The Question

Two variants were proposed:

1. **Own brand** — commission open-mold carbon wheels in China, put a new brand on them, ship worldwide DTC.
2. **Official seller** — become an authorized dealer/distributor for an existing brand.

The premise behind both: Ryet and other unbranded Chinese carbon wheels perform like the big brands at ~1/3 the price, so there is arbitrage available.

**The premise is correct. The conclusion doesn't follow.** That price gap is *already* being captured — by the factories selling direct. It is not sitting unclaimed.

---

## Superseded idea: cycling apparel

Retired for market saturation. Worth noting: the same saturation test applied honestly to wheels gives the **same answer, harder**. Apparel has ~$15–40 COGS and no injury liability. A wheelset has ~$400 COGS and can kill someone at 60 km/h on a descent. Saturation is the *smaller* of the two problems here.

---

## Part 1 — Own Brand: Why This Fails

### 1. The market is more saturated than apparel, not less

Brands already competing on exactly "pro performance, 1/3 the price," all well-reviewed in 2026 roundups: **Winspace, Elitewheels, Yoeleo, Farsports, Light Bicycle, ICAN, BTLOS, Superteam, ProX, Carbonal, Trifox, Toccay, Ryet, Elevate, Tukus, Wheels Drive** — plus every no-name AliExpress listing.

Most of them buy from the same handful of Xiamen-area factories. Daniel's own experience — bought Ryet, bought other unbranded sets, all excellent — **is the evidence that this market is well served.** He is the satisfied customer proving there is no gap.

### 2. A new brand has to be *more* expensive than what Daniel himself buys

The factories sell DTC. Winspace, Elitewheels and Yoeleo run their own storefronts, warehouses and dealer networks. Inserting a new brand between the factory and the rider adds a margin layer and removes nothing. The pitch becomes "same open-mold rim as Ryet, but more expensive, from a brand you've never heard of, with no warranty history." There is no story there.

Differentiating requires a **custom mold** (45–60 day lead time plus tooling cost) — which means real R&D money and real engineering, not a logo.

### 3. Capital: the MOQ math breaks the budget on the first SKU

Factory MOQs run **30–50 rims per size/model**, or **10–20 complete sets per spec**. Road disc needs, at absolute minimum:

| Dimension | Variants |
|-----------|----------|
| Rim depth | 35 / 50 / 65mm |
| Freehub | Shimano HG + SRAM XDR |
| **Minimum SKUs** | **6** |

At 10–20 sets MOQ × ~$400/set factory cost = **$24,000–48,000 in inventory** for a credible opening range. Launching one single depth to economize:

| Line item | Cost |
|-----------|------|
| Inventory (20 sets × $400) | $8,000 |
| Decals, packaging, photography | $500–1,500 |
| EN ISO 4210-7 type testing, independent lab | ~$2,000–5,000 *(estimate — needs quote)* |
| Product liability insurance | *unquoted — see §4* |
| Shopify + site build | $1,000–2,000 |
| Launch advertising | $2,000+ |
| **Total, one model** | **$14,000–25,000+** |

Budget available: **$5,000–10,000**, with a stated preference for staged deployment. This is 2–3× over on a single unvalidated SKU — the exact opposite of "test small, scale what works."

### 4. Liability is the actual dealbreaker

Wheels are a safety-critical structural component. The brand owner — not the factory — is the legally responsible party in essentially every target market.

- **EU:** EN ISO 4210 compliance, CE marking, and an EU-based Authorized Representative.
- **US:** CPSC 16 CFR 1512; ISO 4210 accepted as evidence of compliance.
- **2025/2026 amendments** add post-impact residual-strength testing specifically for carbon composites.

A Colombia-domiciled freelancer with no legal entity in the US or EU cannot realistically obtain product liability cover for carbon wheels sold into those markets, and cannot honor a warranty claim from Belgium. One cracked rim in a bunch sprint is an uninsured personal-liability event against personal assets.

**This is a different risk class from every other idea in this repo.** The coffee subscription's worst case is a refund.

### 5. 2026 tariffs killed the "ship worldwide from China" model

This is the part that has changed recently and invalidates the plan outright:

- **US de minimis ended 29 Aug 2025.** The $800 duty-free threshold is gone — every single parcel is now dutiable. The DTC-single-set-from-China playbook depended on that threshold.
- **Section 301 + IEEPA stack.** July 2026 USTR action put new 10%/12.5% tariffs in place; adult bicycles from China now land around **56%** cumulative.
- **The incumbents already adapted.** Winspace runs a US warehouse advertising "In-Stock / Fast USA Shipping / No Tariffs." Pre-positioning inventory in-market is now the requirement to compete — and that takes capital Daniel doesn't have.

A new brand shipping sets one at a time out of China in 2026 arrives at the customer's door with a surprise duty bill, against competitors shipping domestically from US and EU stock.

### 6. It uses none of Daniel's skills and breaks the lifestyle constraint

Zero dbt, zero BigQuery, zero SQL, zero Python. The work is freight forwarding, customs paperwork, WhatsApp support, warranty disputes and wheel truing.

Worse — the **stated purpose** of any side business here is financial freedom to travel and explore nature full-time. An inventory-and-warranty business ties him to a storage room, a customs broker and a service queue in Bogotá. It funds the goal by making the goal impossible.

## Part 2 — Official Dealer: The Viable Version

First, a correction on framing: **"official seller" and "worldwide" are mutually exclusive.** Dealer agreements are territorial. The brands already sell worldwide themselves; what they grant a dealer is a *geography*. So the real idea is: **authorized dealer for Colombia** (possibly Andean region).

That version is genuinely more interesting, for one reason: **there is a real service gap.**

A Colombian rider buying Ryet or Elitewheels direct from AliExpress gets a great wheel and **no recourse**. Cracked rim at month 8? Shipping a wheelset back to Xiamen from Bogotá is economically irrational. No local warranty, no fitting, no truing, 4–8 week waits, and a customs gamble. That is the gap — not price.

### Dealer programs that exist

| Brand | Dealer channel |
|-------|----------------|
| Yoeleo | Public dealer/distributor application page; published worldwide distributor list |
| Elitewheels | Dealer brochure, actively recruiting dealers and distributors |
| Winspace | Established dealer network, UCI Continental team sponsorships |

Roughly 200+ dealers across North America, Europe, Asia and Oceania — **Latin America is thin.** That is a genuine opening.

### Market context (Colombia)

- ~**440,000 bicycles/year** sold in Colombia.
- Four brands — Trek, Specialized, Giant, Scott — control ~**80%** of the market; HA Bicicletas alone imported 192,155 units (~$15.7M USD).
- High-end sales are **personalized retail**, not big-box — favorable to a small specialist operator.
- Existing high-end carbon players (AGR, ALV/Dar Pedal) prove the segment exists and sustains small brands.

### Landed cost model, per wheelset

| Line | Value |
|------|-------|
| Dealer cost (FOB China) | $750 |
| Freight + broker, consolidated | $80 |
| CIF | $830 |
| Colombian duty, HS 8714.9x @ 5% | $42 **⚠️ unverified** |
| IVA 19% on (CIF + duty) | $166 |
| **Landed cost** | **~$1,037** |
| Local retail price | $1,700 |
| **Gross margin** | **$663 (39%)** |
| Net after fees, marketing, 5% warranty reserve | **~$500–550** |

Pricing sanity check: a comparable Zipp or Roval set retails in Colombia at roughly $2,500–3,500 USD after the same duty stack plus importer margin. **$1,700 with a local warranty, immediate availability and a fitting** is credible against both the Western brands *and* against AliExpress-plus-freight-plus-risk.

### Path to the $2,000/month goal

| Sets/month | Net/month | vs. goal |
|------------|-----------|----------|
| 2 | ~$1,050 | 53% |
| **4** | **~$2,100** | ✅ **Goal** |
| 6 | ~$3,150 | ✅ Exceeded |

**4 premium wheelsets per month** from a one-person Instagram-and-group-ride operation in Bogotá. Plausible in a cycling-obsessed city — not easy.

### The capital trap, and how to dodge it

Stocking inventory to serve walk-up demand means 8–12 sets across depths and freehub standards = **$6,000–9,000 at dealer cost before landing costs.** That consumes the entire budget upfront and violates the staged-investment rule.

**The dodge: presale / consolidated-order model.**

- Customer orders and pays a 50% deposit.
- Orders are batched into a monthly consolidated shipment.
- 3–4 week delivery, communicated upfront as the tradeoff for a price far under the Western brands.
- Daniel's already-owned wheels serve as the demo/test set.

Working capital requirement drops to **near zero.** Per-unit freight is higher and margin somewhat thinner, but downside risk becomes trivial and the model is validated with real money from real buyers before a single set is stocked.

### Startup cost, presale model

| Line item | Cost |
|-----------|------|
| Dealer application + sample set (partly owned already) | $0–800 |
| Landing page + Instagram setup | $200 |
| Colombian import/broker setup, first shipment | $300 |
| Small local ad spend + group-ride presence | $200 |
| **Total** | **~$700–1,500** |

Well inside budget, staged, and reversible.

## Risks (dealer model)

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Location dependency vs. travel goal** | 🔴 High | Partner with an existing Bogotá shop for fulfillment, service and warranty intake. If this can't be delegated, the idea is structurally wrong for Daniel. |
| Brand goes direct in Colombia later | 🟡 Med | Negotiate a defined territory term; build the customer list as the durable asset |
| Colombian customs valuation disputes | 🟡 Med | Use an experienced broker from shipment one; verify HS code with DIAN before quoting anyone |
| Warranty claims eat margin | 🟡 Med | 5% reserve built into pricing; confirm in writing who pays return freight |
| Buyers just order AliExpress direct | 🟡 Med | Sell the service, never the price — warranty, fitting, immediate availability |
| Customer service time load | 🟡 Med | At 4 sets/month it fits 10–15h/week. Past ~8/month it does not. |
| COP/USD swings | 🟢 Low | Price in USD or re-price monthly |

## Decision

**Do not build a wheel brand.** The capital gap is 2–3×, the liability is uninsurable from Colombia, and the 2026 tariff regime broke the ship-worldwide-from-China model that the whole idea rests on.

**The dealer version is testable for under $1,500** — but be clear-eyed that it is a local, physical, service business. It contradicts the full-time-travel goal unless fulfillment and service are delegated to a local shop from day one.

### Smallest viable test — 30 days, ~$0

Before applying to any dealer program or spending anything:

1. **Post a real offer** in Colombian cycling groups, Strava clubs and the Alto de Patios / Patios climb community: specific brand, specific wheelset, specific price, 3–4 week delivery, local warranty.
2. **Ask for a $100 refundable deposit** to hold a slot on the first batch.
3. **Count.** 5+ deposits in 30 days → real demand, proceed to dealer application. Fewer than 3 → the price gap alone isn't a business, and this closes cleanly at zero cost.

Deposits are the only honest signal. Instagram likes from riders who will buy from AliExpress anyway are not.

### A third option worth considering

Daniel has already done the work of researching, buying and comparing multiple Chinese carbon wheelsets. That knowledge is the asset — and unlike wheels, it is:

- **Buildable with his actual skills** — scraped spec database, landed-cost calculator by country including duty and IVA, price and weight and depth comparison dashboards. dbt, Python, BigQuery, Looker. All of it.
- **Zero inventory, zero liability, zero customs.**
- **Fully location-independent** — which is the entire point of the exercise.
- Monetizable via affiliate programs the same brands already run.

Honest caveat: affiliate content monetizes **slowly**. Hitting $2–3k/month in 3–6 months is unlikely. It is a 12–18 month play, not a fast one — and it should be evaluated against the repo's timeline constraint rather than assumed superior.
