# Idea: B2B Specialty Coffee Subscription — Bogotá Offices

## Status
> 🔍 Exploring — economics validated, next step: conversation with Roots Café cousin

## Concept

A new specialty coffee brand (distinct from Roots Café) that sells monthly coffee subscriptions to tech companies, startups, and offices in Bogotá. Roots Café (Casa de Tostión, Chapinero — cousin) acts as the silent roasting partner/supplier at a wholesale rate. Daniel owns the brand, the client relationships, and the commercial operation.

## Why This Works

- **Supply solved from day 1** — cousin's roastery handles production
- **No one targeting premium B2B in Bogotá** — Juan Valdez/Rentokil dominate with commodity, not specialty
- **USD earner in Colombia** — cost structure is favorable
- **Recurring, predictable revenue** — B2B contracts renew monthly

## Unit Economics (baseline)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Avg office size | 15 employees |
| Coffee consumption | ~0.48 kg/employee/month |
| Coffee per office | ~7.2 kg/month |
| Price to office | $160,000 COP/kg |
| Revenue per office | ~$1,152,000 COP/month |
| Wholesale cost (cousin) | $80,000 COP/kg |
| Delivery per office | $20,000 COP |
| Gross margin per office | ~45% |

## Key Assumptions

- 2 cups/person/day × 11g × 22 working days = 0.484 kg/employee/month
- FX rate: 4,200 COP/USD
- Fixed costs: ~$300,000 COP/month (website, accounting, misc)

## Critical Variable

**Wholesale price from cousin** is the biggest lever. Negotiating from $80k → $65k/kg drops break-even from ~7 offices to ~5.

## Risks

- Cousin's roastery capacity may cap scale at ~30 offices
- Bogotá delivery logistics — batch routes to control cost
- Client churn — lock in 3-month minimum contracts
- Coffee commodity price swings — add quarterly price-review clause
